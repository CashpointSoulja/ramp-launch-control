# Ramp Launch Control, explained simply

Imagine a busy school. Kids tell teachers what's wrong ("the lunch line is too slow"), the school plans new things (a new lunch menu), and sometimes things break (the oven stops working).

Ramp Launch Control is the notebook the school helper keeps so nothing falls through the cracks.

**Customer Voice is the suggestion box.** A helper reads every note, puts it in the right pile ("lunch", "buses", "library") and gives it to the teacher who owns that pile, with a sticky note saying why. If a note is too messy to read, or could belong to two piles, the helper asks a grown-up instead of guessing. Piles get bigger stars when many *different* kids complain, and when those kids matter a lot to the school, so one kid shouting fifty times can't take over.

**Launch Readiness is the checklist before a school trip.** Permission slips signed, bus booked, teachers told. If one important box isn't ticked, or the bus driver is sick, the trip is a "no-go" and the list says exactly why. If someone changes the plan after the head teacher signed it, the signature doesn't count any more.

**The Incident Board is the "what broke" board.** When the oven stops, someone writes down how bad it is, who is fixing it and how long it took. Afterwards they ask "why?" a few times until they find the real reason, and write down who will make sure it doesn't happen again. If the same thing breaks twice, the board says so.

The three are connected: the kids' complaints point at what to fix, the trip checklist won't say "go" while the oven is still broken, and the "what broke" board feeds back into the suggestion box.
